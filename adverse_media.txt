Sources- Consider whether the sources are credible.
●
●
●
●
Media type: Internal news agencies and national newspapers are seen as
higher quality sources than regional and local providers.
Content: Impartial opinions enhance credibility. Check for professionalism:
details, sources, footnotes, quotes, website appearance increase credibility.
Number of sources: Credibility increases with more credible sources.
Geopolitical context: Trust politically neutral sources for credibility. Be
cautious of news from countries with limited democracy or press freedom.
Use a risk-based approach for non-credible sources with strong evidence.
Timeline: Consider relevance of Adverse Media alerts based on when the behaviour
occurred. Timing and follow-up incidents impact significance for convictions and
allegations.
Adverse Media related to terrorism does’t expire. In these cases you must also
submit a SAR.
●
●
●
Allegations within 5 years are considered. If there’s no investigation or
conviction for more than 5 years, they may be deemed unfounded or
irrelevant for action. Similarly investigations cases older than 5 years without
updates may be deemed immaterial or of little risk to not warrant any attention
or further action. Updates would be detected in ongoing monitoring.
Consider prosecutions and convictions within a 10-year timeframe.
Convictions in the last decade remain relevant for assessing risks, including
reputational and money laundering risks, as well as the likelihood of fine
repayment. Beyond 10 years, without new Adverse Media, we prioritise
financial inclusion to maintain relationships.
Expired or irrelevant disqualifications/bans, like a former banned director now
working as an employee.
If the relevant time has passed and there's no subsequent Adverse Media, we
respect the concept of financial inclusion and continue the relationship.
